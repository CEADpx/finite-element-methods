\begin{frame}{From a rate to an error estimate}
Chapter 7 ended with an a priori statement such as
\[
  \norm[a]{u-u_h}\leq C_I\sqrt{k_1}\,h^{s-1}
  \seminorm[H^s(\Om)]{u}.
\]
It predicts a rate, but it does not answer two questions after a computation:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Is the error on this mesh small enough?
  \item Which elements should be refined?
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{0.25cm}
Chapter 8 replaces the unknown derivative of $u$ by defects computed from
$u_h$:
\[
  \boxed{\text{solve}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{estimate}\ \longrightarrow\
  \text{mark}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{refine}.}
\]
Today: derive the residual, localize it to elements and facets, and build the
estimator. Marking and refinement follow in the next class.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The residual is what the discrete solution leaves unsatisfied}
For the diffusion problem, let
\[
  a(w,v)=\int_\Om k\grad w\cdot\grad v\,\dd x,
  \qquad
  \ell(v)=\int_\Om fv\,\dd x-\int_{\GamN}qv\,\dd s.
\]
The exact and discrete problems are
\[
  a(u,v)=\ell(v)\quad\forall v\in\Vzero,
  \qquad
  a(u_h,v_h)=\ell(v_h)\quad\forall v_h\in\Vhzero.
\]
Define the residual functional
\[
  \boxed{R(u_h;v)=\ell(v)-a(u_h,v),\qquad v\in\Vzero.}
\]
With $e=u-u_h$,
\[
  \boxed{R(u_h;v)=a(e,v).}
\]
Therefore $R(u_h;v_h)=0$ for every $v_h\in\Vhzero$: the residual vanishes on
the test space used to compute $u_h$, but generally not on all of $\Vzero$.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{In the energy norm, residual size equals error size}
Because $a(\cdot,\cdot)$ is an inner product,
\[
  |R(u_h;v)|=|a(e,v)|\leq\norm[a]{e}\,\norm[a]{v}.
\]
Taking the supremum gives one inequality. Taking $v=e$ gives equality:
\[
  \boxed{
  \norm[a]{e}
  =\sup_{0\neq v\in\Vzero}
  \frac{|R(u_h;v)|}{\norm[a]{v}}.}
\]

\vspace{0.25cm}
This identity is exact, but it is not yet computable:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the maximizing direction is $v=e$, which is unknown;
  \item the supremum ranges over the infinite-dimensional space $\Vzero$;
  \item the single number does not yet say where the error is located.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.18cm}
The next step rewrites $R(u_h;v)$ as element and facet contributions.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Elementwise integration by parts exposes the strong defect}
Start from
\[
  R(u_h;v)=\int_\Om fv\,\dd x-\int_{\GamN}qv\,\dd s
  -\int_\Om k\grad u_h\cdot\grad v\,\dd x.
\]
On one element $\mathcal K_e$,
\[
  \int_{\mathcal K_e}k\grad u_h\cdot\grad v\,\dd x
  =-\int_{\mathcal K_e}\divg(k\grad u_h)v\,\dd x
  +\int_{\partial\mathcal K_e}(k\grad u_h\cdot\bn_e)v\,\dd s.
\]
After summing over elements,
\[
\begin{aligned}
  R(u_h;v)
  ={}&\sum_e\int_{\mathcal K_e}
  \bigl(f+\divg(k\grad u_h)\bigr)v\,\dd x\\
  &-\sum_e\int_{\partial\mathcal K_e}
  (k\grad u_h\cdot\bn_e)v\,\dd s
  -\int_{\GamN}qv\,\dd s.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first line is the elementwise balance defect. The second line must be
regrouped facet by facet.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Facet regrouping gives two flux defects}
\small
Define the computed flux $\bq_h=-k\grad u_h$. On an interior facet,
the two element-boundary terms combine into
\[
  j_{\mathcal F}
  =-[\![k\grad u_h\cdot\bn_{\mathcal F}]\!]_{\mathcal F}
  =[\![\bq_h\cdot\bn_{\mathcal F}]\!]_{\mathcal F}.
\]
On a Neumann facet,
\[
  j_{\mathcal F}
  =-\bigl(q+k\grad u_h\cdot\bn\bigr)
  =\bq_h\cdot\bn-q.
\]
On $\GamD$, $v=0$, so there is no residual term. With
\[
  r_e=f+\divg(k\grad u_h)=f-\divg\bq_h,
\]
the exact representation is
\[
  \boxed{
  R(u_h;v)=
  \sum_e\int_{\mathcal K_e}r_e v\,\dd x
  +\sum_{\mathcal F\in\Fh^{\mathrm{int}}\cup\Fh^N}
  \int_{\mathcal F}j_{\mathcal F}v\,\dd s.}
\]
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What the residuals measure}
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[
  box/.style={draw,rounded corners=2pt,align=center,text width=3.15cm,
    minimum height=1.25cm,inner sep=5pt},
  >=Stealth]
  \node[box,fill=blue!6] (element) {\textbf{Element residual}\\
    $r_e=f-\divg\bq_h$\\balance defect inside an element};
  \node[box,fill=orange!9,right=0.45cm of element] (int) {\textbf{Interior facet}\\
    $j_{\mathcal F}=[\![\bq_h\cdot\bn_{\mathcal F}]\!]$\\flux leaving one element does not enter the next};
  \node[box,fill=green!9,right=0.45cm of int] (neu) {\textbf{Neumann facet}\\
    $j_{\mathcal F}=\bq_h\cdot\bn-q$\\computed flux misses prescribed flux};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

For $p=1$ and elementwise-constant $k$, $\grad u_h$ is constant on each element,
so $\divg(k\grad u_h)=0$ and $r_e=f$. The computed gradient information then
appears in the facet jumps.

\vspace{0.2cm}
Galerkin orthogonality balances the two kinds of defects on every free basis
function $\phi_i$:
\[
  \sum_e\int_{\mathcal K_e}r_e\phi_i\,\dd x
  +\sum_{\mathcal F}\int_{\mathcal F}j_{\mathcal F}\phi_i\,\dd s=0.
\]
Neither residual must vanish separately.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Activity: residuals for the uniformly loaded bar}
Let $L=AE=f=1$, $\overline P=0$, and use a uniform mesh of size $h$ with
linear elements. The exact solution is
\[
  u(x)=x-\frac{x^2}{2},
\]
and Chapter 7 showed $u_h=\mathcal I_hu$.

\vspace{0.2cm}
Work with a neighbor. For element $\mathcal K_e=(x_e,x_{e+1})$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Find $u_h'|_{\mathcal K_e}$ using the chord slope of $u$.
  \item Compute
  \[
    r_e=f+(AEu_h')',\quad
    j_i=(AEu_h')(x_i^+)-(AEu_h')(x_i^-),\quad
    j_{N+1}=\overline P-(AEu_h')(L^-).
  \]
  \item For an interior hat function $\phi_i$, verify that its element and
  facet residual contributions cancel.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{0.15cm}
The nodal values are exact. Predict: do all residuals vanish?
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Activity debrief: exact nodes do not imply zero residual}
The chord slope equals the exact derivative at the midpoint $\bar x_e$:
\[
  u_h'|_{\mathcal K_e}=u'(\bar x_e)=1-\bar x_e.
\]
Therefore
\[
  \boxed{r_e=1,\qquad j_i=-h,\qquad j_{N+1}=-\frac h2.}
\]
Every residual is nonzero, although $u_h(x_i)=u(x_i)$ at every node.

\vspace{0.25cm}
For an interior hat function,
\[
  \int_0^1 r\phi_i\,\dd x=h,
  \qquad
  j_i\phi_i(x_i)=-h.
\]
At the last node, the two terms are $h/2$ and $-h/2$. This is residual
orthogonality in local form: the discrete equations balance element and facet
defects, but the defects still detect the error between nodes.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Galerkin orthogonality supplies the powers of $h$}
The residual representation alone contains $\norm[L^2(\mathcal K_e)]{e}$,
which gives no local mesh-size weight. For any $v_h\in\Vhzero$,
\[
  R(u_h;v)=R(u_h;v-v_h).
\]
Choose a local approximation $v_h=\mathcal Q_hv$. Hypothesis (Q) requires
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \norm[L^2(\mathcal K_e)]{v-\mathcal Q_hv}
  &\leq C_Q\,h_e\seminorm[H^1(\omega_e)]{v},\\
  \norm[L^2(\mathcal F)]{v-\mathcal Q_hv}
  &\leq C_Q\,h_{\mathcal F}^{1/2}
  \seminorm[H^1(\omega_{\mathcal F})]{v}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $\omega_e$ and $\omega_{\mathcal F}$ are fixed-size patches. The
operator maps $\Vzero$ into $\Vhzero$ and uses local averages, so it is
defined for $H^1$ functions even when nodal interpolation is not.

\vspace{0.15cm}
The factors $h_e$ and $h_{\mathcal F}^{1/2}$ become the estimator weights.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Local indicators assign a computable number to each element}
\small
For each element $\mathcal K_e$, define
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\eta_e^2={}&h_e^2\norm[L^2(\mathcal K_e)]{r_e}^2
+\frac12\sum_{\substack{\mathcal F\subset\partial\mathcal K_e\\
\mathcal F\in\Fh^{\mathrm{int}}}}
h_{\mathcal F}\norm[L^2(\mathcal F)]{j_{\mathcal F}}^2\\
&+\sum_{\substack{\mathcal F\subset\partial\mathcal K_e\\
\mathcal F\in\Fh^N}}
h_{\mathcal F}\norm[L^2(\mathcal F)]{j_{\mathcal F}}^2.
\end{aligned}}
\]
The global estimator is
\[
  \boxed{\eta=\left(\sum_e\eta_e^2\right)^{1/2}.}
\]
The factor $1/2$ assigns half of an interior facet to each adjacent element.
After summing over elements, every facet is counted exactly once. A Neumann
facet belongs to only one element, so it receives no factor $1/2$.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{In one dimension, reliability has an explicit constant}
For the bar with variable $AE(x)\geq(AE)_0>0$, nodal interpolation is defined
for every $H^1$ error. The nodal terms vanish for
$w=e-\mathcal I_he$, leaving only element residuals. The interpolation bound
and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\[
  \boxed{
  \norm[a]{u-u_h}
  \leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{(AE)_0}}
  \left(\sum_eh_e^2\norm[L^2(\mathcal K_e)]{r_e}^2\right)^{1/2}.}
\]

For the activity, $(AE)_0=1$, $r_e=1$, $N=1/h$, and
\[
  \eta_{\mathrm{1D}}^2=Nh^3=h^2,
  \qquad \eta_{\mathrm{1D}}=h.
\]
Chapter 7 gave $\norm[a]{e}=h/\sqrt{12}$, so
\[
  I_{\mathrm{eff}}
  =\frac{\eta_{\mathrm{1D}}}{\norm[a]{e}}=\sqrt{12}\approx3.46.
\]
The estimator decreases at the correct rate but overestimates by a fixed
factor on this mesh sequence.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Multidimensional reliability: statement and proof route}
Assume Hypothesis (Q), shape regularity, and $k\geq k_0>0$. Then
\[
  \boxed{\norm[a]{u-u_h}\leq C_{\mathrm{rel}}\eta,
  \qquad C_{\mathrm{rel}}
  =C_Q\sqrt{\frac{N_{\mathrm{ov}}}{k_0}}.}
\]
The proof uses four steps:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\norm[a]{e}^2=R(u_h;e)=R(u_h;e-\mathcal Q_he)$;
  \item insert the element-and-facet residual representation;
  \item apply Hypothesis (Q), then Cauchy--Schwarz to the combined sums;
  \item use finite patch overlap and
  $\seminorm[H^1(\Om)]{e}\leq\norm[a]{e}/\sqrt{k_0}$.
\end{enumerate}
The constants are independent of $h$, but $C_Q$ and $N_{\mathrm{ov}}$ are
not normally computed. Thus $\eta$ controls the error up to a fixed factor;
its displayed value is not automatically a certified numerical upper bound.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Definition, reliability, efficiency, and effectivity differ}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.28}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.18\linewidth}p{0.75\linewidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{Term} & \textbf{Claim} \\
\midrule
Estimator & $\eta$ and $\eta_e$ are computable definitions; a definition alone
does not compare them with the error. \\
Reliability & $\norm[a]{e}\leq C_{\mathrm{rel}}\eta$. A small estimator implies
a small error. \\
Local efficiency & $\eta_e\leq C_{\mathrm{eff}}
(\norm[a,\omega_e]{e}+\mathrm{osc}_e)$. A large indicator should correspond
to error on a nearby patch, up to data oscillation. \\
Effectivity & $I_{\mathrm{eff}}=\eta/\norm[a]{e}$, evaluated when the exact
error is known, measures the numerical scale of the estimator. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.25cm}
Today we proved the one-dimensional reliability result and stated the
multidimensional result under Hypothesis (Q). Efficiency is a desired
property and a definition here; no efficiency theorem is being claimed for
this candidate estimator without a matching source and precise oscillation
definition.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What to retain and what comes next}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The residual is the error measured through the bilinear form:
  $R(u_h;v)=a(u-u_h,v)$.
  \item Elementwise integration by parts separates element balance defects from
  interior and Neumann flux defects.
  \item Galerkin orthogonality allows subtraction of a local approximation;
  this produces the powers of the local mesh size.
  \item $\eta_e$ localizes the estimator and $\eta$ combines the elements.
  Reliability means the estimator controls the energy error up to a
  mesh-independent constant.
\end{itemize}
